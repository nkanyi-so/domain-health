# 0010: two sets of cases, a rules-only baseline, tighter definitions

**Status:** accepted, 2026-11-19. Changes [0008](0008-evals.md) in part. Changed in part by [0012](0012-the-claim-says-what-is-measured.md) the same day: the comparison with the baseline is evidence and not a bar, the claim no longer rests on it, and nothing below that says the open cases show judgment, that no loosened table passes (true only of the one family of tables the test tries), or that work stops if the model does not beat the baseline, still holds.

**Context.** The first measure was twenty cases labelled from the rubric's bands. Once the measure was written, an independent challenge, and before it a review, each produced about 130 lines of if/else from `evals/RUBRIC.md` that reproduced every label. A rules engine with no model would have scored full marks, so passing the bars would have shown that the model follows a table, not that it makes the "judgment-call" the claim names. The challenge also found that the bars were coarse (27 expected items, four risk types expected once), that "harmful" missed the sixth to eighth risks a handled click brings into view, that output dropped by validation had no defined score, and that nothing stopped a prompt being re-run until it passed.

**Decision.** I chose to keep the claim's wording and make the measure able to show it.

- **Two sets.** Thirty table cases, whose labels are the bands and are re-derived by a test, and ten open cases, where the bands do not settle the matter and the label is a written call with its reason. Every risk type is expected in at least two cases.
- **Who writes the open cases.** I will not hand-label. An agent with fresh context that never writes, sees or tunes the prompt drafts them, each with a short reason grounded in public deliverability guidance where it applies (the bulk-sender requirements published by Google and by Yahoo, M3AAWG's sender best practices, the SPF, DKIM and DMARC RFCs). I review them before any prompt exists. The README says where the labels came from: drafted by AI against written guidance and reviewed by the engineer who built the original system, not by a deliverability specialist.
- **Held out.** Whoever writes or changes a prompt does not read the open cases, any summary of them, or their per-case results. Only their totals are reported. While the measure was set up, the case files were never opened and were checked by test only, but the drafting agent's one-line summary of each case was seen while the change was written up. So the prompt is written and changed by an agent with fresh context that is given the rubric and the table cases and nothing else, and it is passed table-case results only.
- **A rules-only baseline.** The bands applied by code (`backend/src/domain_health/evals/table.py`) are scored with the same harness and reported beside the model, for the table set and the open set. On the open set the model does better than the baseline when it finds on average at least one more expected item per run than the baseline, its harmful count is no higher, and its precision is at least 0.80. (The baseline's precision there is 1.00 by construction, which a test keeps true, so precision is held to its bar, not to the baseline. As the cases stand, the baseline finds 9 of the 14 expected items on the open set, with nothing harmful, so the model needs at least 30 of 42 over three runs.) If it does not, work stops and the weaker wording is proposed: "judgment-call risks picked by a model from a closed list and scored against a written rubric".
- **Definitions.** Harmful counts everything returned, up to eight. Dropped output found nothing for coverage, never matches for stability, and is counted beside the measures. Stability is a share, at least 0.90. Coverage is also reported per risk type. "Harmful 0" is written as "0 in 120 case-runs". Precision is kept although it adds little on the table cases.
- **Rules for paid runs.** The numbers judged against the bars are one run of the final prompt version; every attempt is reported; an unchanged prompt is never re-run to pass; a prompt does not quote the rubric's thresholds.
- **Checks in code.** Table labels and fix lists re-derived from the rubric; an open label must depart from the bands; validators refuse an input that cannot happen; a test, added with the model, that only a case's `input` reaches it; a test, added with the recordings, that no recording for a seeded client names a type the bands call wrong.

**Consequence.** The bars are still judged on all forty cases, where thirty can be passed by following a table. What shows judgment is ten cases, which is a small number: one open case is a tenth of that evidence, and the comparison with the baseline can turn on one or two calls. The open labels are AI-drafted calls, signed off by me, on what a specialist would say; a specialist might disagree with some. `table.py` now exists in the source tree and could be misused as a filter or as prompt material; it is listed as part of the measure and its docstring says what it may be used for. A paid eval run is 120 calls, twice the first estimate: about $0.06 on `gpt-6-luna` and about $0.70 on `gpt-6.1-terra`, budgeted at $0.10 and $1.00. The worst case for the whole build rises from under $3.50 to under $6, still below the $10 cap. Revisit if the open set proves too small to separate the model from the baseline, or if a specialist reviews the labels.

## Added the same day, after the second challenge

**Context.** A second challenge to the measure above showed that the comparison with the baseline could be passed by a table with one threshold moved: every item the baseline missed on the ten open cases was one the bands already call "acceptable". Returning the acceptable complaint band as well found 10 of 14, the pass mark, with nothing harmful. Four of the ten open calls could not move any score and three could only count against the model.

**Decision.** I chose to add harder open cases and set a limit of one round.

- **Eighteen open cases.** Eight more, and two of the first ten redrafted, by a fresh agent under the same rules. Two kinds were asked for: calls that expect a risk the bands call wrong or do not list, meant to be out of a loosened table's reach; and calls that make wrong a risk the bands call acceptable, meant to punish loosening. (The first kind turned out to be within reach of a table with one number moved: see decision 0012.) The set is forty-eight cases; "0 in 120 case-runs" becomes "0 in 144".
- **The rule, in code** (`backend/src/domain_health/evals/comparison.py`): no more harmful output than the baseline; precision at least 0.80; on average at least one more expected item per run; and in at least two of three runs a real gain that comes from at least two open cases.
- **No loosened table passes.** A test runs the baseline plus the bands' "acceptable" for every subset of the thirteen types through that rule and requires each to fail. Other tests: one good run of three is not enough, a gain from one case is not enough, one harmful answer fails it, and every open note names the types its call moves.
- **The limit.** This is the last round on the measure. If a further challenge still says a change is needed on claim against measure, there is no further round: the weaker wording is applied ("judgment-call risks picked by a model from a closed list and scored against a written rubric"), the sentence that the open cases show more than a table is dropped, the cases and the baseline are kept as evidence, what was tried and why it fell short is recorded for the README's limits, and the checks are run again. The same fallback applies if the model does not beat the baseline on the paid eval runs: the wording is proposed and work stops for my decision.
- **The claim test.** It is now written down what the claim test does: it checks that the exact sentence is present, and cannot see a different wording beside it.

**Consequence.** A paid eval run is 144 calls; the worst case for the build is under $7 against a cap of $10. The comparison is harder to pass by accident, and cannot be passed by a table that also returns the bands' "acceptable" types. It can still be passed by a table with one threshold moved, which the third challenge showed; the limit above then applied, and decision 0012 records the outcome. It also rests on eighteen AI-drafted calls, which a specialist might dispute.
